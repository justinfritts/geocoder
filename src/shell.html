3. Geocode

This applies only to places that could not be found. A row that names no city already resolves to its state or country centre regardless of this setting, because there is nothing else it could mean.

Vastly larger is not “pick the biggest”: a candidate qualifies only at 50,000 people and twenty times the next. That resolves Busan, KOR — a city of 3.3 million against a hamlet in Jangseong-gun — while leaving Springfield, USA ambiguous, because Missouri, Massachusetts and Illinois are 167,882, 153,606 and 116,565 and there is no honest way to choose.

Only one recorded as inhabited goes further and is a real guess. A population of zero in GeoNames means not recorded, not uninhabited: 84% of US places have none on file, and Moorestown NJ is one of them despite housing 14,000 people. It settles about half of all ambiguous rows — GREENWOOD, MS resolves to Leflore County at 15,431 rather than the Itawamba namesake — and it can pick wrong wherever the real town simply has no census record. Rows decided by either rule say which one in the Status column.

On Chrome and Edge you will be asked where to save first, and the result is written to that file as it is produced rather than held in memory. That is what makes a multi-million row file possible — and why re-running one needs the file dropped in again. Other browsers keep the result in memory and offer a download at the end.

Five columns are added to your file: Latitude, Longitude, Matched Place, Match Type and Status. A row that could not be placed comes back with empty coordinates and the reason in Status — never with a guess.
